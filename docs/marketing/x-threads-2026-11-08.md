## 线程 1 — AI 数据中心的隐藏水账是肉眼所见的 12 倍

🧵 Everyone talks about AI's power bill. Nobody talks about its water bill.

We ran the numbers on US data centers. The result is absurd.

(1/8)

2/ The headline number: US data centers used ~66 billion liters of water on-site in 2023.

Sounds like a lot. It's the tiny number.

3/ Because generating the *electricity* for those data centers used ~800 billion liters at the power plant.

That's a 12x multiplier nobody puts on the slide.

4/ "Closed-loop cooling" gets cited as the fix. But closed-loop only closes the on-site loop.

The 92% of the water — the part at the power plant — was never in the loop to begin with.

5/ So every "we're water-neutral" claim that only counts the campus is counting the 8% and waving at the 92%.

The physics don't care about the press release.

6/ Why we even care: OMC is a decentralized GPU network. Compute that's physically distributed inherits the grid it sits on — including this water math.

You can't route around it by building a bigger campus.

7/ The real lever is using the GPUs you already have, where the power already exists, instead of stacking idle capacity in one drought-prone county.

That's a market-design problem. We're building the marketplace for it.

The constraint is geography + trades, not silicon.

## 线程 3 — 800V 正在让数据中心用了十年的 54V 母线排退休

🧵 Data centers are quietly rewriting their power math.

The 54V busbar — the standard for a decade — is being retired by 800V.

Here's the arithmetic. (1/8)

2/ High-density racks now pull so much per-GPU wattage that the *last meter* of delivery becomes the bottleneck.

Not the chip. The copper feeding it.

3/ Busway losses and conversion stages scale with per-GPU power. At old densities it was noise. At AI densities it's a real, paid-for tax on every watt.

4/ 800V DC cuts the conversion stages between the grid and the GPU. Fewer stages = less loss = more of the watt you paid for reaches the silicon.

5/ This is why "build cost" for an AI rack is now a power-delivery problem, not just an electrical one.

The last meter decides margins.

6/ OMC nodes plug into this reality instead of pretending it's someone else's problem. Compute that prices delivery honestly beats compute that hides it.

7/ Full rack-power breakdown, with the loss curves, on the forum.

## 线程 4 — 智能体不会用信用卡：机器和机器之间怎么付钱？

🧵 Your AI agent can book a flight, write code, and query a model.

It cannot open a credit card.

So how do agents pay each other? (1/8)

2/ The entire agent economy assumes machines can transact. Today they can't — they borrow a human's payment rail and hope.

That's not a market. That's a hack with a timeout.

3/ Machine-to-machine (M2M) payments need three things: an address that isn't a person, a settlement that's instant, and a unit both machines agree on.

Crypto is the only stack that has all three natively.

4/ OMC is built around this. A job is escrowed, computed, verified, and settled — machine to machine, no human in the loop per transaction.

5/ "Verified" is the part everyone skips. An agent will happily pay for garbage output. Per-job verification is what makes M2M payments safe to automate at scale.

6/ This is the unglamorous plumbing that the agent economy runs on. No verification, no trust, no economy — just expensive mistakes at machine speed.

7/ We're shipping the settlement + verification layer now, not "researching it for 2028."

## 线程 5 — 推理按结果计费，不按 GPU 小时

🧵 You don't pay a taxi by the hour if it gets lost.

So why do we bill AI inference by GPU-hour? (1/8)

2/ GPU-hour pricing charges you for the *machine's time*, not the *answer*. Idle spin-up, retries, a model thinking itself in circles — all on your tab.

3/ "Result billing" flips it: you pay for verified output, not for silicon sitting warm.

If the job fails verification, you don't pay for the attempt.

4/ This matters because inference — not training — is where AI spend is heading. Always-on agents call models millions of times. Hourly pricing explodes at that volume.

5/ Per-job verification is what makes result billing possible. You can only bill for results you can prove.

6/ OMC does exactly this: escrow the job, a node runs it, verification passes, payment releases. No output, no charge.

7/ It's also why decentralized beats a single vendor here — competition on verified results, not on who locks you into their hour.
