Full · 120 words

Omniverse Compute (OMC) is a decentralized compute network on BNB Smart Chain that matches GPU operators with the people who need compute. Operators stake OMC as collateral — a bond sized in dollars, not in tokens, so it keeps its weight when the price moves — register their hardware, and execute jobs. Requesters pay in OMC, with a 10% discount for paying in OMC rather than in stablecoins. The protocol takes 8% of settled volume: 50% burned, 30% to staked operators, 20% to the treasury. Total supply is fixed at 1,000,000,000 with no inflation parameter, and new issuance is capped by trailing 30-day fee revenue, so a network that earns nothing issues nothing. Mainnet and token generation are scheduled for 1 January 2027.

How to write about it without being wrong

A short list, offered because these are the errors that show up most often in coverage of this category:

  • It is a network, not a coin. OMC is the unit that settles work on it. The interesting claim is the fee flow, not the ticker.
  • BEP-20, not ERC-20. OMC is issued on BNB Smart Chain.
  • There is no price. OMC is not listed anywhere and is not sold by the project. Any figure quoted as "the OMC price" is invented.
  • The airdrop is free to join. Three social actions and a wallet signature. A separate 0.01 BNB per entry is charged at claim time, inside the claim transaction — a processing charge, not a purchase, and not a discount on OMC.
  • Say "testnet" when describing what runs today. Mainnet, the token contract, the audit and any listing are scheduled for 1 January 2027 or later.
  • Staking APR is a division, not a promise. It is annual emission divided by total staked, capped at 100%, and it is zero until the network earns fees. Do not quote an APR as a yield.

Airdrop, stated correctly

20,000,000 OMC — 2% of supply — in 1,000,000 slots of 20 OMC each, first come first served. Registration is open now and free: follow, retweet and comment, join Telegram, then connect a BNB Smart Chain wallet and register up to five base entries (plus one per invited wallet that registers, up to five more). Claiming opens with mainnet on 1 January 2027 and closes 31 March 2027; unclaimed slots return to the protocol. Machine-readable terms: /airdrop.json. Full announcement: /news/omc-airdrop-is-open.

Attributable statements

Two paragraphs that can be quoted directly and attributed to the Omniverse Compute team. They are positions, not marketing lines.

"We priced the emission schedule against fee revenue rather than against a calendar, because a schedule can only answer how fast a pool may be spent. It cannot answer whether the network is earning anything while it spends. If there is no revenue, there is no emission — a network that never finds demand simply never inflates."
"The airdrop is free and we say so on the page, in the announcement and in the machine-readable feed. The 0.01 BNB per entry is a processing charge at claim time and it is published before anyone connects a wallet, because the alternative — a number discovered at the wallet prompt — is how airdrops earn a reputation they never recover from."
