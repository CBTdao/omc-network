What we are building

Omniverse Compute (OMC) is a decentralized GPU compute network. We aggregate idle and under-used GPU capacity — from independent node operators, small data centres and community hardware — and route it to workloads that need it: AI training and inference, rendering and scientific simulation.

The network is designed around an open marketplace where committed capacity supplies guaranteed availability, and open capacity sells the hours nobody reserved. That structure, and the pricing it produces, is documented in our whitepaper and tested publicly on our testnet.

What is live today

  • Public testnet on BNB Smart Chain — verified test contracts, published RPC endpoints and a node onboarding guide on the Testnet page.
  • Whitepaper v2.0 — architecture, verification and slashing rules, matching engine and full tokenomics, on the Whitepaper page.
  • Open-source repository — website and documentation sources under an MIT licence at github.com/CBTdao/omc-network.
  • Community airdrop — opens November 1, 2026 (2% of supply, free to claim, no purchase and no payment ever required). Details on the Airdrop page.
  • Companion sites — AITop for AI tool comparisons and the DCF forum for long-form compute research.

Mainnet and compute mining are planned after the testnet phase; the current roadmap is published on the News page and in the whitepaper.

How we work

We publish the engineering roadmap and the repository in public, and we ship in small increments that anyone can verify. Where we make a factual claim about pricing, performance or market structure, we publish the methodology alongside it so it can be checked.

Editorial standards

Our research and review content follows three rules, and we apply them to every piece we publish:

  • No paid placements. Rankings and reviews are not sold, and no brand pays us for a position.
  • No affiliate links. We do not earn commissions from the products we compare.
  • Methodology in the open. Where we compare tools or rates, we state the criteria, the date and the sources.

We write about the crypto and AI industries, including projects we do not work with, and we publish critical conclusions about them when the evidence supports it.
